# Q668 companion: pure CVaR at any common interior level, with binary rewards

**Theorem.** For each rational q strictly between zero and one, deciding feasibility of simultaneous lower-tail CVaR constraints at the single common level q on coordinatewise liminf mean payoff is ∃ℝ-complete, even with binary state rewards. The reduction is polynomial uniformly in the bit length of q. The model has rational transition probabilities and arbitrary randomized history-dependent strategies. This strengthens checked result #37, not a separate catalog solve.

## Replace all expectations by CVaR at the same specified level

Start from Q668's hard instance with old expectation and half-tail CVaR constraints and integer state rewards in [0,R], R≥1. Duplicate a payoff coordinate once per constraint if necessary. Set w=min(q,1−q)>0. Add one forced initial transition: probability w enters the original initial state, and the three remaining probabilities q−w, w/2 and 1−q−w/2 enter absorbing sinks S1, S2 and S3. All masses are nonnegative and sum to one; zero-mass branches can be omitted. Put reward 0 at S1 and R at S3 in every coordinate. Put R at S2 for an expectation coordinate, and 0 for a half-tail CVaR coordinate.

Write CVaR_q(X)=q⁻¹∫₀^q F_X⁻¹(t) dt. The new lower q fraction for an expectation coordinate consists of q−w mass at zero plus the entire w mass of the old coordinate law. Thus the new CVaR equals (w/q) E[X]. For an old half-tail coordinate it consists of q−w/2 mass at zero plus half the old branch, so the new CVaR equals (w/(2q)) CVaR_(1/2)(X). Select these positive scale factors for the respective thresholds. Atoms at zero or R can be selected in either order, with no change in the integrals. The forced original branch has exactly the original feasible conditional strategies: its added history is fixed and its finite rewards vanish in the liminf mean. At q=1/2 the first sink is unused, yielding precisely masses (1/2,1/4,1/4).

## Replace bounded integer state rewards by binary rewards

For every original state s in the padded model, create a forced sampling state, an emission state indexed by (s,z), and a controller state indexed by s. In the sampling step draw a binary vector Z with conditional means r_j(s)/R. A single uniform variate and threshold r_j(s)/R in each coordinate implement this abstractly; implement it in the finite MDP by sorting the distinct breakpoints together with 0 and 1, with one outcome per positive interval, assigned that interval's rational length. There are at most d+1 outcomes at each s. The emission state rewards Z and has a forced exit to the controller state. The controller state has the original selection of actions and the original successor probabilities, redirected to successor sampling states. The sampling and controller rewards are zero. Each old step is thus exactly three new steps. Long-run payoffs of the absorbing branches are handled in the same way, by looping these blocks.

Let s_i be the state sampled in block i and let H_i be the history through its arrival, before drawing Z_i. For each coordinate, D_i=Z_(i,j)−r_j(s_i)/R lies in [−1,1], is measurable by H_(i+1), and has conditional expectation zero given H_i. The partial sums are martingales even if future actions depend on past emission vectors. Azuma–Hoeffding gives tails at εn bounded by 2 exp(−ε²n/2), so Borel–Cantelli for a countable sequence of positive ε gives n⁻¹∑_(i<n)D_i→0 almost surely. Since there are finitely many coordinates this holds simultaneously. Boundary partial blocks change the average by o(1). Thus the entire liminf mean-payoff vector is the vector on the projected state sequence divided by 3R, almost surely. Scale all thresholds again by 1/(3R).

Any original strategy lifts by ignoring the extra observations. Conversely, for an expanded strategy, the projected process is implementable by an original randomized strategy. On the original state sequence use simulated emissions with the same conditional sampling law, then select the same action; each old-state transition conditional on that action is the same. Alternatively, use the action distribution of the expanded policy conditional on the visible projected history, which reproduces every finite-dimensional projected state/action distribution by induction and defines a strategy even without explicit private-memory variables. This addresses adaptive use of the binary emissions. Finite state/transition descriptions and threshold bit lengths grow polynomially.

Membership in ∃ℝ is the delivered Q668 upper bound, with no expectation constraints. The original paper already addresses pure CVaR in general, so the claim here is the exact class and hardness under the simultaneous fixed interior level and binary-reward restrictions, not the invention of a pure-CVaR model. We do not claim hardness for fixed dimension, restricted transition alphabets, deterministic strategies, VaR, or q=1.
